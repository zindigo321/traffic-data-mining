# Full-Data Experiment Method and Data Contract

This translated provenance record describes the historical full-run archive. Relative references to its evidence, results, and figures use that archive layout.

Full data covers all source records obtained and validated against their hashes and schema within the existing New York project scope of 121 directed road segments and two peak periods, 07:00–09:00 and 17:00–19:00. The 52 historical shards and two continuation shards are recorded individually in `source_snapshot`. Original speeds are in mph. Ten-minute bins use America/New_York civil time. Source shards are filtered using 0 < speed ≤ 85, travel time > 0, and the fixed road-network rules. The status field alone does not exclude valid speeds. The source rows and filtering counts are retained. All fixed grid cells receive predictions, and unknown labels remain part of coverage reporting. [Official NYC DOT data](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/d/i4gi-tjb9)

Labels retain the original two-bin rule. Each bin requires two qualifying five-minute half-bins. The historical mean uses at least three qualifying previous days for the same road, time bin, and day type. A positive requires both consecutive bins to be below 40% of their respective available historical means. Insufficient observations or history, or low speed without consecutive confirmation, is labeled `unknown`. The new protocol removes the wait and coverage requirements for starting the experiment while keeping the original ground-truth calculation. The first peak-period bin can lack a valid `lag20` because there is no consecutive earlier speed observation. It still receives a score and is reported in the missing group.

Development training is [05-11, 08-03) and development validation is [08-03, 08-31). Final training is [05-11, 08-31), calibration is [08-31, 09-28), and retrospective testing is [09-28, 11-12). Final training covers 112 days with 171672 known windows, 4649 positives, and 167023 negatives. Calibration covers 28 days with 47780 known windows. Testing covers 45 days with 80536 known windows. Every date falls within training or subsequent evaluation. As training extends, historical means update daily using only past observations then available. Calibration and testing share the frozen training history. The earlier public holdout participates in the retrospective evaluation. The report records that the test dates were public.

The model scores target bin t ten minutes before it starts. The latest dynamic inputs are from [t−20, t−10) or earlier. Speeds in [t−10, t) and [t, t+10) appear only on the ground-truth side. The original V7 `forecast_inputs` generates 52 admissible features. The same cutoff applies to central and adjacent segments. Historical road transition statistics update only on training days and remain frozen during calibration and testing. The source table has no independent observation arrival time, so `data_as_of` serves as the offline availability timestamp.

The Graph Transformer reuses multihead attention with a directed structural mask. Nodes are directed road segments. The fixed endpoint geometry and turn-angle constraints create 56 candidate directed edges, and 54 nodes are isolated. Local node 0 is the target. Its one-hop subgraph has at most five valid nodes and a separate padding mask. Inputs have shape [B,N,104], formed by 52 values imputed and scaled on training data and 52 missingness indicators. The model has hidden width 32, two layers, and four heads, with attention logits of shape [B,4,N,N]. Targets attend to their physical predecessors and themselves. The mask prevents attention across graphs and batches. Attention cost per layer is O(BN²D), with N ≤ 5 on this fixed graph. An empty graph is rejected, and a single-node graph runs normally.

The pooled and target node vectors are concatenated into a 64-dimensional classifier input. The classifier produces one logit per road segment and ten-minute bin. Mean computes sum(valid_i h_i)/sum(valid_i), Max takes the elementwise maximum across valid nodes, and Attention uses a weighted sum with weights softmax(wᵀh_i+b). All three pooling methods have cost O(ND). Attention adds 33 parameters. Each pooling model is trained and evaluated independently. The core source is `project/v10/v9/v8/v7/v6/v5/v4/v3/v2/v1/model.py`.

The five full-data recipes visit each known training sample exactly once per epoch. The step-matched sampled center-only control uses 12000 negatives and all positives, cycling through the same random permutation until it matches the full-data number of visits. All six recipes consequently take the same number of steps per epoch. The control records its unique IDs and repeat counts explicitly. Training uses 12 epochs, batch size 128, a learning rate of 0.001, seeds 11/22/33, and two CPU threads per job. The center-only ranking recipe adds the original V10 softplus of the negative-minus-positive logit difference within the admissible low-speed group. It uses the highest-scoring quarter of that group's negatives and a weight of 0.35. Logistic regression uses the same admissible center features, training-set scaling, and all known training samples.

Development AP alone selects checkpoints. For each recipe, final training saves the median selected epoch across the three development seeds and still executes all 12 epochs. The primary pooling model is selected by mean development AP across three seeds, with constrained F1 and registration order breaking ties. Test metrics are computed only after the final models and thresholds are frozen. Each final neural model is compared with the deterministic baselines on exactly the same test keys.

The primary operating point maximizes F1 on calibration with budgets of ≤12 total FP/day and ≤6 recovery FP/day over complete calendar days. The unconstrained maximum-F1 calibration threshold shows the trade-off. False positives are counted by road-segment window. Adjacent ten-minute positive windows on a road are merged into events. AP uses `average_precision_score`. ROC-AUC, balanced accuracy, positive-class F1, and confusion counts are also reported. Metrics are N/A when classes are insufficient. Equal recall at 50/60/70/80% holds TP counts fixed and sorts scores in descending order, using the key to break ties. The primary point is 60%, evaluated separately on the full support and the admissible low-speed group. Paired intervals use 1000 shared calendar-day block draws.

Raw shards and feature arrays are stored externally and read-only. The package supplies their hashes and rebuild entry points. NPZ files use `allow_pickle=False`, and weights use `weights_only=True`. Synthetic samples are for engineering tests. Real models and results are listed separately. New York speed estimates, road coverage, inferred geometry, and source timestamps can introduce bias. The original Geolife coursework and Chicago results under their original definitions are reported separately.
